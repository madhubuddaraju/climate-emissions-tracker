## Findings

1. **The ten hottest years on record are all since 2015.** 2024 was the
   warmest at +1.53 °C. The 10-year average is now +1.28 °C.
2. **At the post-1975 trend, the long-term average crosses 1.5 °C around 2033.**
   This is a straight-line extrapolation of +0.21 °C/decade, not a climate model projection.
3. **Temperature tracks CO₂ closely.** Across 67 years, each additional 100 ppm of CO₂ lines up with
   +1.09 °C of warming (R² = 0.93). Correlation alone isn't proof of cause, but it matches the
   physics: CO₂ traps outgoing heat.
4. **CO₂ is accumulating faster, not slower.** Concentration now rises about
   2.5 ppm a year, roughly 3.0x the 1960s rate.
5. **Emissions are still rising globally** (+1.3% in 2024).
   Since 2015 Asia's annual emissions changed by +4.3 Gt while Europe (-0.7 Gt)
   and North America (-0.5 Gt) cut theirs. China emits
   32% of the world total; India is up +453% since 1990.
6. **41 countries cut CO₂ while growing GDP** between 2010 and 2022
   (absolute decoupling), including United States, Japan, Germany, Canada, Mexico.
7. **Per-person emissions vary 9x among the largest emitters**: from
   2.2 t (India) to
   20.4 t (Saudi Arabia) per person.
